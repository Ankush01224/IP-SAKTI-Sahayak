# US FDA Botanical Drug Guidance & Ayurvedic Regulatory Pathways

## Section 1 - Classification of Ayurvedic Formulations in the United States
In the United States, Ayurvedic products can enter the market through two distinct statutory regulatory pathways under the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act):
1. **Dietary Supplements (DSHEA 1994)**:
   - Formulations marketed without disease treatment, prevention, or mitigation claims.
   - May carry structure/function claims (e.g., "supports joint mobility", "promotes natural vitality"), accompanied by mandatory FDA disclaimer.
   - Must comply with 21 CFR Part 111 (Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Packaging, Labeling, or Holding Operations for Dietary Supplements).
2. **Botanical Prescription Drug (CDER Botanical Guidance)**:
   - Intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent a disease.
   - Requires submission of an Investigational New Drug (IND) application and New Drug Application (NDA) approved by the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER).

## Section 2 - Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) for Polyherbal Botanical Drugs
1. **Batch-to-Batch Consistency**: Because Ayurvedic formulations frequently consist of multiple complex botanical extracts, standard active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) single-molecule assays are insufficient.
2. **Multi-Marker Fingerprinting**: The applicant must establish spectroscopic and chromatographic chemical fingerprints (HPLC, HPTLC, LC-MS) tracking multiple characteristic phytochemical markers across multiple production harvests.
3. **Raw Botanical Material Controls**: Rigid specifications for raw botanical substances including taxonomic authentication, geographical origin, agricultural practices, post-harvest drying, and residual solvents.

## Section 3 - European Union (EMA) Traditional Herbal Medicinal Products Directive (THMPD)
Under European Directive 2004/24/EC, Ayurvedic medicinal products with at least 30 years of documented safe traditional medicinal use (including at least 15 years within the European Union) may qualify for a simplified Traditional Herbal Registration (THR) without extensive phase III clinical trials, subject to European Pharmacopoeia quality monographs.
