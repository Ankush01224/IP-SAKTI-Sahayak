# The Patents Act, 1970 (Act No. 39 of 1970)

## Section 3(p) - Traditional Knowledge Non-Patentability
An invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components is not an invention within the meaning of this Act.

### Statutory Interpretation & Ayush Guidelines
1. **Classical Formulations**: Any formulation, herb, recipe, or therapeutic method described in classical Ayurvedic texts—including Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Ashtanga Hridaya, Sharangadhara Samhita, and Bhavaprakasha—is deemed to be in the public domain and constitutes established prior art. No patent can be granted for such classical formulations.
2. **Novel Combinations and Section 3(e)**: A substance obtained by a mere admixture resulting only in the aggregation of the properties of the components thereof, or a process for producing such substance, is not patentable under Section 3(e). For an Ayurvedic formulation to be patentable, the applicant must demonstrate non-obvious synergistic therapeutic efficacy beyond the individual known properties of the herbs.
3. **Requirement under Section 10(4)(ii)(D)**: If the patent specification mentions biological material sourced from India, the applicant must disclose the source and geographical origin of the biological material and declare whether approval from the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) has been obtained.
4. **Revocation under Section 64**: A patent granted in contravention of Section 3(p) or based on undisclosed Indian traditional knowledge is liable to be revoked under Section 64(1)(p) of the Patents Act, 1970.
