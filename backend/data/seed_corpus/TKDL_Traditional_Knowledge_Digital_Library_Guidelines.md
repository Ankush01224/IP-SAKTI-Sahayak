# Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) Guidelines & Defensive Protection

## Chapter 1 - Institutional Architecture & Mandate
The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is a pioneering Indian digital repository established jointly by the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) and the Ministry of Ayush, Government of India. Its mission is to prevent the misappropriation of Indian traditional medical knowledge through defensive protection in international patent offices.

## Chapter 2 - Database Coverage & Taxonomic Classification
1. **Corpus Scope**: Contains over 450,000 formulations from ancient classical texts of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, and Sowa-Rigpa.
2. **Traditional Knowledge Resource Classification (TKRC)**: An innovative classification system mirroring the International Patent Classification (IPC), enabling patent examiners worldwide to search Ayurvedic concepts (like Vata, Pitta, Kapha, Rasa, Bhasma, Taila) in 5 international languages: English, French, German, Japanese, and Spanish.
3. **Classical Source Texts**: Scanned, verified, and mapped from texts including Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Ashtanga Sangraha, Sharangadhara Samhita, Chakradatta, and Bhaishajya Ratnavali.

## Chapter 3 - International Access Agreements & Biopiracy Prevention
1. **Access Agreements with Patent Offices**: CSIR and Ministry of Ayush have entered into formal Access Agreements with major intellectual property authorities:
   - United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO)
   - European Patent Office (EPO)
   - Japan Patent Office (JPO)
   - United Kingdom Intellectual Property Office (UKIPO)
   - Canadian Intellectual Property Office (CIPO)
   - Intellectual Property Australia (IP Australia)
2. **Defensive Pre-Grant Interventions**: TKDL monitoring teams examine published international patent claims that attempt to patent known properties of Indian medicinal plants (such as Curcuma longa/Turmeric, Azadirachta indica/Neem, Phyllanthus emblica/Amla, Withania somnifera/Ashwagandha). Formal third-party prior art observations are submitted to cause rejection or voluntary withdrawal of spurious biopiracy claims.
3. **Access for R&D Entities**: Under Cabinet approval, non-patent access to TKDL is expanded to academic institutions, researchers, and domestic startups under strict Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) to spur evidence-based R&D while preserving state defensive sovereignty.
