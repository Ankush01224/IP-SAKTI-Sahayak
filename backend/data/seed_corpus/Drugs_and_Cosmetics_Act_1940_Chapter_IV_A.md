# Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 & Rules 1945

## Chapter IV-A - Provisions Relating to Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs

## Section 33A - Application of Chapter IV-A
This Chapter shall apply only to Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani (ASU) drugs. Under Section 3(a), an Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug includes all medicines intended for internal or external use for or in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease or disorder in human beings or animals, manufactured exclusively in accordance with the formulae described in the authoritative books of Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani systems specified in the First Schedule.

## Section 33C - Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board (ASUDTAB)
The Central Government shall constitute a Board to be called the Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board to advise the Central Government and State Governments on technical matters arising out of the administration of this Act.

## Rule 158-B - Guidelines for Issue of License with respect to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Drugs
1. **Classical ASU Drugs**: Manufactured strictly as per the recipes in authoritative classical texts listed in Schedule 1 of the Act. For classical drugs, no safety or clinical efficacy trial data is required provided the method of manufacture, indications, and dosages conform verbatim to the classical text.
2. **Patent or Proprietary (P&P) ASU Medicines**: Defined under Section 3(h) as a formulation containing ingredients mentioned in the authoritative books of Ayurveda, but manufactured with new combinations or novel dosage forms.
   - For novel combinations of ingredients with prior documented usage: Evidence of safety from published scientific literature, acute toxicity studies, and textual references.
   - For ASU drugs with new indications (claims different from classical texts): Pilot clinical trial evidence as per Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines for ASU drugs is mandatory.
3. **Schedule T - Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)**: All manufacturing units of Ayurvedic drugs must strictly comply with Schedule T standards regarding factory premises, plant hygiene, air handling, water quality, raw material standardization, batch manufacturing records, and quality control laboratory testing.
